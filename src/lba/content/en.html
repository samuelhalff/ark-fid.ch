New AMLA rules

Since 1 October 2026: advisors (law firms, fiduciaries) subject to the revised Swiss AMLA, and reporting of beneficial owners to the transparency register.

Apply matter by matter, when the file is opened and whenever its purpose changes. The type of service (opinion, call, contract review) decides nothing; only the underlying transaction counts.

New matter

  1. Is the matter contentious?

    Art. 2 para. 4 let. f AMLA. To be safe, no presumption: transactional advice does not become procedural because a dispute is possible.

    • Judicial, administrative, criminal or arbitral proceedings
    • Advice to prepare, conduct or avoid proceedings, assessment of litigation risk
    • Enforcement of the outcome of proceedings

    If no: next step

    If yesOutside AMLA

  2. Does the matter involve a listed transaction?

    Art. 2 paras. 3bis and 3ter AMLA

    • Purchase or sale of real estate
    • Formation of a NOE in Switzerland, or of any entity seated abroad
    • Management or administration of a NOE
    • Contributions to and distributions by a NOE
    • Purchase or sale of an entity through a NOE
    • Domiciliation of an entity at the firm for more than 6 months

    If yes: next step

    If noOutside AMLA

    Employment contract, commercial lease, distribution, T&C, NDA, general tax advice, formation of a Swiss operating company, share deal between operating companies. If the operating character is in doubt: grey zone.

  3. Does your advice causally contribute to the transaction?

    Art. 12d AMLO. Phone calls and written advice are treated the same. The scale below is our reading; when in doubt, apply the stricter tier.

    Outside AMLAGeneral information on the law, without reviewing the client's situation
    Grey zoneRecommendation tailored to the client: covered if it contributes causally to the transaction; the only doubt is that link, assessed when the advice is given
    AMLAImplementation details, structuring of contributions, drafting of deeds

    If yes or Grey zone: next step

    Grey zoneIn dubio pro AMLA

    Identify the client as soon as the discussion becomes specific, not when the memo is delivered.

  4. Is there a financial transaction?

    Art. 2 para. 3bis AMLA, including the organisation of funds; art. 12e AMLO for real estate. Transaction existing or planned according to the FDF commentary; a purely abstract analysis is not covered.

    • Sale price, capital payment, contribution in kind, distribution, loan
    • Real estate: as soon as the parties have declared their intention to conclude the sale (also: equivalent act, usufruct or building right granted for payment)
    • Cash Separate regime for dealers: due diligence from the first franc received in cash in a real estate sale (art. 8a para. 4 AMLA); an advisor is caught only if it is itself a dealer (art. 13 AMLO)

    If yes: next step

    If noOutside AMLA

    Rare in practice for listed transactions. The act does not define the term: to be safe, when in doubt, assume there is a financial transaction.

  5. Does an exception apply?

    Art. 2 paras. 4bis and 4ter AMLA; art. 2 para. 3 let. a AMLO for intra-group. Main exceptions:

    • Transfer of real estate or an entity below CHF 5 million, price paid and received exclusively through a bank or a regulated FI. A cash payment defeats the exception; to be safe, so does payment in kind, set-off or a vendor loan.
    • Purchase of residential property in Switzerland for own use or as a replacement; to be safe, do not rely on it if own use is uncertain (second home, later letting)
    • Family law, matrimonial property, inheritance, gifts, related persons; foundation upon death
    • Officer of an operating entity, public-benefit foundation or operating association seated in Switzerland
    • Notarisation without ancillary advice; intra-group services; audit by a licensed auditor; agricultural transfers

    If no: next step

    If yesOutside AMLA for this matter

    The internal AMLA unit certifies in writing, in the file, the exception and the evidence of its conditions.

  6. Is the professional basis threshold met?

    Art. 12f AMLO, one criterion is enough. The thresholds relate only to the covered advisory activity, not to the firm's whole practice; for a firm organised as a legal entity, they are probably added up at firm level (our reading).

    • More than CHF 50,000 of AMLA gross revenue per calendar year
    • More than 20 clients or 20 transactions per calendar year
    • More than CHF 5 million of third-party assets at any one time
    • More than CHF 2 million of transactions per calendar year

    If yes: result below

    If noBelow the thresholds: caution

    Advice remains professional if it is an independent activity aimed at lasting income (art. 12f para. 1 AMLO). To be safe, if such matters are regular or paid: treat the activity as professional, apply due diligence, file the application to join and keep a register of AMLA matters. Deadline (art. 12g): two months from moving to a professional activity to file the application with an SRO or declare the activity to the supervisory body; until then, existing clients only.

Advisor under the AMLA

  • Application to join to be filed before 1 December 2026 (AMLO transitional provision). According to the Geneva Bar Association, the SRO SAV/SNV is currently the only SRO accepting lawyers as advisors (to be checked with the SRO). Collective membership of the firm recommended.
  • Until the decision on the application, advisor activity is limited to existing business relationships: no AMLA matter from a new client.
  • The lawyer responsible for the matter is a member or registered. Associates, trainees and paralegals may be auxiliaries (art. 2 para. 3 let. b AMLO) under five cumulative conditions: careful selection, instructions and controls by the advisor; integration in its organisational measures (art. 8d AMLA), initial and ongoing training; acting only in its name and on its behalf; paid by it and not by the end client; written agreement. Otherwise the person may itself be an advisor: to be safe, treat it as one.
  • Report to MROS: a lawyer must report only if they carry out a financial transaction in the name or on behalf of the client and the information is not covered by professional secrecy (art. 9 para. 2 AMLA, art. 321 SCC). As long as the lawyer only advises, no report. If either condition is in doubt, to be safe: do not carry out the transaction, clarify immediately with the SRO without disclosing any secret covered by art. 321 SCC, and report to MROS without delay once the conditions of art. 9 para. 2 are met, without informing the client or third parties (art. 10a para. 5).

Territorial scope: activity carried out in or from Switzerland (art. 2 para. 1 AMLO); to be safe, regardless of the client's domicile or the location of the asset. Start from the service requested. Each branch ends either out of scope or with an AMLA result; in that case, go through the two common filters at the bottom.

  1. Does an exception apply to the matter?

    Art. 2 paras. 4bis and 4ter AMLA; art. 2 para. 3 let. a AMLO for intra-group. Main exceptions:

    • Transfer of real estate or an entity below CHF 5 million, price paid and received exclusively through a bank or a regulated FI. A cash payment defeats the exception; to be safe, so does payment in kind, set-off or a vendor loan.
    • Purchase of residential property in Switzerland for own use or as a replacement; to be safe, do not rely on it if own use is uncertain (second home, later letting)
    • Family law, matrimonial property or inheritance transactions, gifts, transactions between related persons
    • Foundation created by disposition upon death
    • Officer of an operating entity, public-benefit foundation or operating association seated in Switzerland
    • Intra-group services; audit by a licensed auditor; notarisation without ancillary advice; agricultural transfers and land consolidation

    If no: next step

    If yesOutside AMLA for this matter

    Document in writing, in the file, the exception applied and the evidence of its conditions.

  2. Is the activity carried out on a professional basis?

    Art. 12f AMLO, one criterion is enough

    • Gross revenue above CHF 50,000 per calendar year
    • More than 20 clients or more than 20 transactions per calendar year
    • Third-party assets above CHF 5 million at any one time
    • Transactions above CHF 2 million per calendar year
    • Or, failing that, activity aimed at lasting income

    If yes: result below

    If noBelow the thresholds: caution

    To be safe, if such matters are regular or paid: treat the activity as professional (art. 12f para. 1), apply due diligence, file the application to join or declare the activity, keep a register of matters. Deadline (art. 12g AMLO): two months from moving to a professional activity.

Advisor under the AMLA

  • Fiduciary already a member as FI: notify the advisor activity to its SRO before 1 December 2026 (AMLO transitional provision, para. 2), after checking that it registers advisors.
  • Otherwise: application to join an SRO within the same deadline (para. 1).
  • From 1 October 2026: identification of the client and beneficial owner, purpose of the transaction, documentation (arts. 8b and 8c AMLA), internal organisation (art. 8d AMLA).
  • Recommended practice, following from arts. 8b to 8d AMLA: keep a register of AMLA matters and assess the risk of each.
  • Reasonable suspicion: immediate report to MROS (art. 9 para. 1ter AMLA), without informing the client or third parties (art. 10a para. 5). The exception for lawyers and notaries does not apply to a fiduciary.

The duty lies with the client company

Filing with the register is the company's own duty, through the most senior member of its management body (art. 12 TJPG). That person may delegate the task but remains responsible. Neither the law firm nor the fiduciary has a filing duty of its own.

Major exception: if you are that member, the duty is yours. That member is the chair of the executive management or, where there is no separate management, of the board of directors or of the administration (in our reading and to be safe, also a sole director or sole manager); the liquidator in a liquidation, the administrator in a composition moratorium; all of them where the function is held jointly (art. 20 paras. 3 and 4 TJPV).

But clients should be advised

No law requires you to inform clients. It is nonetheless prudent:

  • many companies take 1 October 2026 as the deadline, when it is the start of the deadlines;
  • an inaccurate entry may be flagged by the bank, required from 1 April 2027 to report persisting discrepancies (FI);
  • identifying the BO, preparing the documents and filing on EasyGov are a service to offer.

Key facts

Who is a BO

The natural person who ultimately controls the company by holding, directly or indirectly, at least 25% of the capital or votes, or in another way; failing that, the most senior member of the management body (art. 4 TJPG). Definition distinct from that of the AMLA.

Duties of BOs

Report their status to the shareholder or partner concerned, or directly to the company where control runs through a chain, then any change within one month (art. 14 TJPG). The shareholder or partner reports the beneficial owner to the company within one month of control arising, and any change within one month of learning of it (art. 13).

Filing

Online on EasyGov, free of charge. Any change must be filed within one month of the day the company learns of it (art. 10 TJPG).

The register

Kept by the Federal Office of Justice. Not public and declaratory (arts. 20 and 23 TJPG). Each company can obtain a confirmation or an extract of its own data.

Penalty

Fine of up to CHF 500,000 for intentional breach of the filing duties (art. 43 TJPG); up to CHF 100,000 for failing to comply with a final decision (art. 44).

Record keeping

Supporting documents kept for ten years after the person ceases to be a BO (art. 8 TJPG). The list kept under the old law by AG/SA and GmbH/Sàrl is kept until 1 October 2036 (art. 50).

For you

Advisors and FIs may consult the register for their due diligence (art. 27 TJPG).

From 1 April 2027, an FI reports to the register, within 30 days, a discrepancy that casts doubt on the accuracy, completeness or currency of the data and remains after raising it with the client, except for a company not yet registered that confirms it benefits from the two-year period (arts. 30 and 54 TJPG). Advisors have no such duty.

The register does not release you from identifying the BO yourself (arts. 4 and 8b AMLA); if that check reveals no anomaly, you may rely on the entries (art. 23 para. 2 TJPG).

Real estate Cash

Another change on 1 October 2026: a dealer who receives cash in a real estate transaction is subject to AMLA due diligence, whatever the amount (art. 8a para. 4).

Concepts that tip a matter

With no practice or case law yet, these are where the risk of error lies.

Non-operational

Entity not formed or run to operate or support the operating business of an enterprise or group, in particular a domiciliary company (art. 2a para. 6 AMLA). Abbreviated NOE.

According to the FDF commentary, a holding company may be non-operational depending on its activities, in particular a passive holding that only holds participations. In principle covered, depending on their actual activities (our reading and legal writing): personal holding company, a family's private investment company, private-equity holding structure, passive holding company for real estate or securities, domiciliary company; when in doubt, treat as non-operational. Board mandates of domiciliary companies, however, fall under the existing FI regime; those of other non-operational entities, to be safe, under the advisor regime. In principle not covered: a holding company that actually supports the business of an operating group (management, financing), provided its operating character is documented; when in doubt, to be safe, treat it as non-operational. Mixed cases (SME holding that also accumulates cash): in dubio pro AMLA.

Abroad

Formation of any entity abroad is covered, whether operating or not. The NOE test applies only to Swiss entities.

Causality

Any advice that contributes causally to the transaction (art. 12d AMLO). Advice alone is enough: you need not handle the funds.

According to the FDF commentary: it is enough that the activity furthers the transaction; examples: advice on legal or accounting arrangements, drafting deeds, due diligence report, entry in a register, opening a bank relationship; a purely abstract analysis with no link to a concrete transaction is not covered; the assessment is made ex ante, when the advice is given. Our reading: the form is irrelevant (call, email, memo).

Financial transaction

Movement of assets, existing or planned according to the FDF commentary, linked to the transaction: price, capital payment, contribution, distribution, loan. The organisation of funds is included (art. 2 para. 3bis AMLA). Term not defined by the act.

Professional basis

Any one of the four thresholds is enough (art. 12f AMLO): CHF 50,000 of gross revenue, 20 clients or 20 transactions, CHF 5 million of third-party assets, CHF 2 million of transactions. The activity can be professional even below the thresholds.

A single Geneva property sale often crosses the CHF 2 million threshold on its own. An indication according to the FDF commentary: how the services are presented (website); staying below the thresholds is no safe harbour.

6 months

Duration threshold for domiciliation (art. 2 para. 3ter AMLA). Beyond it, providing an address or premises is covered, whatever the entity.

Report to MROS

The advisor reports immediately to MROS, in particular if it knows or presumes, on reasonable suspicion, that the assets involved in the transaction or service are linked to money laundering, a felony or a qualified tax offence, a criminal or terrorist organisation or the financing of terrorism, or if it breaks off negotiations for that reason (art. 9 para. 1ter AMLA).

Reasonable suspicion: a concrete sign or indications that clarifications do not dispel (paras. 1quinquies and 1sexies). Lawyers and notaries: only if they carry out a financial transaction for the client and outside professional secrecy (para. 2). No informing the client or third parties (art. 10a para. 5). No liability for a report made in good faith (art. 11). The advisor may end the relationship at any time and notifies MROS (art. 9b). The asset freeze (art. 10) applies only to FIs. Failure to report: fine of up to CHF 500,000, or CHF 150,000 for negligence (art. 37).
